## Mathematical assessment

The old midpoint/triangle rule switch at each internal cell edge is absent. The new rule clips one convex emitter domain and partitions its x projection into columns. For positive-area support, each column's area and x moment vary continuously with the clipping planes; its centroid remains within the column. The vertical interval at that centroid is the intersection of the rectangle and both affine half-planes. For nondegenerate support, its endpoints vary continuously through ordinary vertex/column topology changes. Thus a bounded continuous integrand gives a continuous weighted sum. This is continuity, not a guarantee of continuous derivatives or exact integration.

At the full-face limit, each column has area `4/N`, its x centroid approaches the original midpoint, and its N vertical midpoint samples approach the original rows. The per-point multiplier approaches one. Full-path row-major versus partial-path column-major accumulation can still differ by ordinary floating-point summation roundoff. At empty support, area weights tend to zero; undefined centroids of zero-area columns are skipped. This suppresses the bounded contribution regardless of the limiting sample position.

The weight normalization is correct. If normalized column area is `A`, `twiceArea = 2A`, so the returned multiplier is `A*N/4`. Multiplying the prepared `I/N²` candela gives `I*A/(4N)` per row; N rows give `I*A/4`. Summing columns retains the actual illuminated fraction of the square's area four. There is no renormalization of a thin positive region to full-emitter intensity.

Both shading and geometric horizons constrain the domain; the per-contribution geometric check remains. Visibility rays and material evaluations use the redistributed position. A geometric horizon is a visibility mask, so no claim of temporal continuity extends to arbitrary occlusion callbacks, discrete shadow transitions, or discontinuous inputs. Uniform vertical sampling at a column's x centroid is still quadrature: exact area/x moment does not imply exact integration of a kinked vertical first moment, inverse-distance lighting, or a sharp GGX lobe.

## Fixed buffer challenge and concrete rounding case

I ran two deterministic Python binary64 transcriptions of the clipping arithmetic in the isolated A018 directory. They cover **176,916 plane pairs and 3,048,704 column clips**, including exact dyadic boundaries, near corner tangencies, almost coincident/co-directed horizons, and almost opposing horizons at ulp-scale separation. The maximum polygon lengths were **[5, 6, 6, 6]** after first horizon, second horizon, left column, and right column clipping. **No eight-element buffer overflow witness was found.**

The comment that strict sign crossings avoid duplicate corners is nevertheless too strong in finite precision. With the initial square and the normal plane

`(center, x, y) = (6.245004513516506e-17, -0.12500000000000008, -0.1250000000000001)`,

the first clipped polygon is

`[(-1,-1), (1,-1), (1,-0.9999999999999993), (-1,1), (-1,1)]`.

An interpolated intersection rounds onto the retained corner, giving two identical vertices even though neither sign-crossing endpoint was classified as exactly zero. This is a concrete counterexample to the comment, **not** a crash or positive-area weighting counterexample: the repeated vertex contributes a zero-area fan triangle. Root has been notified. The exact convex-polygon bound and finite stress probes do not constitute a universal binary64 capacity proof; I make no such claim.

## Continuity checks, tests, and acceptance limits

A separate 20-case bounded probe varied two active horizons through full/empty, vertex, and almost opposing band configurations with the smooth integrand `exp(0.7*x-0.3*y)`. Perturbations decreased from `1e-6` to `1e-8` to `1e-10`; output differences decreased toward zero, with the largest final difference `1.0547518414227852e-10`. Evidence: `a018/continuity.py` and `a018/continuity-result.json`. This is an independent reasoning/transcription probe, not execution of Swift or a photometric reference test.

The proposed XCTest file preserves the old three-cut counterexample and adds whole-face/topology, signed-slope/off-center, narrow-strip/corner, actual-area, visibility, material, geometric-horizon, and metadata controls. I reviewed these assertions but did not run them. They cover selected error bounds, not all receiver/material configurations; they do not certify positive flux at sub-ulp support widths or universal relative accuracy near zero.

The nearest-face unsupported guard still executes before dark/backface rejection, and per-point 5 cm rejection remains. No new unsupported-surface bypass was found. At most `N²` contribution callbacks are emitted per rectangle; extra clipping/centroid work remains. Cancellation checks remain every eight pixel evaluations, with no check within one light evaluation. This preserves the pixel-count scheduling contract, not a wall-clock cancellation bound or device performance gate.
