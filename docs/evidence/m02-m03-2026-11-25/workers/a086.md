## Defect and exact expectations

Let `t = Double.leastNonzeroMagnitude = 2^-1074`. For the supplied valid source,
subject `(-t, 1.5, t)`, camera `(-t, 1.5, 3)`, light `(64, 2, -64)`, destination
`(100, 1.5, 0)`, rotation 45 degrees, and no mirror, the evaluated coefficients are:

```text
c = 0x1.6a09e667f3bcdp-1
s = 0x1.6a09e667f3bccp-1
c - s = 2^-53
```

The exact affine expression over these **computed binary coefficients** is
`100 + 64 * (c - s) + t * (c - s) = 100 + 2^-47 + 2^-1127`.
The positive final term puts the value above the half-ULP boundary, so the nearest
binary64 result is `100.nextUp` and the arrangement must reject it. The prior
two-product decomposition rounded both tiny products to `t` and both FMA residuals
to zero, losing the term that decides the direction of final rounding.

Changing the subject to `(t, 1.5, -t)` gives the valid counterpart
`100 + 2^-47 - 2^-1127`, which rounds to 100. A zero subject gives the exact tie,
which also rounds to 100. Changing the valid counterpart's light x to `64.nextUp`
adds `c * 2^-46`, giving a genuine outside result that must still reject.
Python `Fraction` calculations confirmed these identities and results.

This work does not claim exact real-valued sine, cosine, or camera reflection
coefficients. It retains the existing coefficient calculations and evaluates the
resulting finite binary64 polynomial without losing its underflowed product tails.

## Implementation

The ordinary arrangement path keeps its existing product expansion and affine
sum. `scaledTerms` now inspects the integer significands of each nonzero product.
After removing trailing zeros, a factor is an odd integer times a power of two.
If the sum of the two powers is below -1074, the exact product contains a bit that
no binary64 residual can represent. That flags the position for recomputation.
Both multiplication passes are checked, including multiplication of a retained
rotation residual by a general reflection coefficient. Factors zero, one, and
minus one keep their existing signed-swap shortcuts.

The fallback recomputes each horizontal coordinate from the original coordinates,
the entered destination, and the existing rotation/reflection coefficients. It
expands to the destination plus at most eight signed products of at most three
finite binary64 inputs. It does not reuse the already rounded tiny products.

For these rare coordinates:

1. Decompose each factor into an odd integer significand, binary exponent, and
   sign. Multiply the at-most-53-bit significands with `multipliedFullWidth` into
   at most three `UInt64` limbs; this operation loses no bits.
2. Align each product at the lowest product exponent. Sum positive and negative
   magnitudes separately, with checked carry propagation. Subtract the smaller
   magnitude from the larger, retaining the exact sign and every cancellation bit.
3. Select the binary64 rounding lattice: retain at most 53 significand bits for
   normal values, and never use a lattice finer than `2^-1074` for subnormals.
   Use the first discarded bit, all remaining discarded bits, and retained parity
   for round-to-nearest-even. This handles both halfway directions, signed zero
   from a nonzero exact value, and a carry from subnormal into normal range.
4. Convert the retained integer to `Double` exactly, then scale by its power of
   two. The preceding rounding makes this scale exact for every finite result.
   An exact zero returns positive zero, matching the existing affine sum.

Camera and y-coordinate translations continue to use the existing addition-only
affine sum. Coefficients, validation boundaries, roll handling, and every standalone
translation/rotation/mirror API remain unchanged. There is no epsilon, clamp,
special-case fixture result, or rejection of small but valid inputs.

## Bounds and guards

- There are at most nine terms and at most three binary64 factors per term;
  both counts have an explicit precondition. Finite-factor preconditions also
  protect the fallback's internal decomposition.
- Each nonzero input's lowest set-bit exponent is at least -1074. Therefore the
  lowest possible product bit is -3222, and each product significand has at most
  159 bits.
- Valid source coordinates have magnitude at most 100. Rotation coefficients
  have magnitude at most 1; the computed reflection ratios are bounded by 2.
  Thus transformed source products remain small. Even an unsupported but finite
  entered destination has its highest set bit no higher than exponent 1023.
- Four additional bits suffice to add nine magnitudes. The largest allocated
  bit span is `1024 - (-3222) + 4 = 4250` bits, which fits in 67 `UInt64` limbs.
  Capacity is computed from the actual products and checked to be in `1...67`.
- Every nonzero carry write checks the array index. Magnitude subtraction checks
  that no final borrow remains. The retained integer is checked to fit within
  53 bits before rounding, and at most one rounding increment is applied.
- Bit reads used during rounding return zero outside the actual magnitude, which
  permits values far below the smallest subnormal without indexing beyond the
  buffer. A smallest-value input is not a reason to reject the scene.

These are domain bounds for this private arrangement helper, not an unbounded
arbitrary-precision facility.

## Added regression coverage

1. **Scene boundary:** the supplied positive-tail counterexample, the valid
   opposite-tail case, an exact tie, and a true overhang. Both signs and both
   horizontal axes are exercised. The outside stored-aim case is isolated from
   a light whose final position is valid.
2. **Rotation plus general reflection:** exact subnormal results for seven small
   coordinate pairs, with both signs, with and without the camera `(3, 4)`
   reflection. Positions and explicit aims are checked by bit pattern, including
   signed zeros. The reflection cases exercise three-factor products.
3. **Normal/subnormal rounding:** exact half-subnormal values with even and odd
   retained significands, each side of those ties with a `+/-2^-1127` tail, and
   the largest-subnormal/minimum-normal boundary. Both signs and explicit aims
   are checked by bit pattern.
4. **Full capacity:** an evaluated sine of `t` and reflection cross coefficient
   `2*t` produce a polynomial term at `2^-3221`. A valid tiny result is accepted;
   greatest-finite destinations on either horizontal axis and at either sign
   exercise the full 67-limb span before normal scene-boundary rejection.

## Recurrence prevention

An FMA residual is exact only when that residual is representable. Tiny finite
inputs can decide a large boundary result after cancellation. Cover product
underflow, subnormal parity, and a real outside neighbor when testing compensated
arithmetic; a small absolute-error check cannot detect this acceptance defect.
