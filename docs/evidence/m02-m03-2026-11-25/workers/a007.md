## Production candidate

Owned changes: `Sources/LightingCore/ReferenceStudy.swift`, `App/StudyKernel.h`, and new `Tests/LightingCoreTests/GrazingStudyContinuityTests.swift` (nine methods).

For each partially illuminated rectangle, clip the whole emitter to the shading hemisphere and, on CPU mannequin receivers, the actual geometric hemisphere. Divide the resulting x projection into 8 or 32 equal columns. Each column carries its exact clipped polygon area and uses its area-centroid x coordinate; 8 or 32 uniform vertical samples lie in its positive interval. Each ray receives the actual column area divided by the row count. This retains at most the existing **64 / 1,024 incident/material/visibility evaluations per emitter**. It adds bounded clipping/area arithmetic, not extra lighting rays.

Area, centroid, and interval endpoints vary continuously under ordinary nondegenerate clip motion. At the fully illuminated limit the positions and weights converge to the original midpoint grid. Fully front-facing sources preserve the original positions and accumulation order. Discrete occlusion sampling still has its usual shadow-boundary transitions; no universal visibility-continuity claim is made.

Original per-point material response, emitting cosine, finite shadow endpoint, geometric-hemisphere check, up-front nearest-emitter near-field guard, point 5 cm guard, and cancellation schedule remain. The shared shader mirrors the one-hemisphere mapping. Four CPU buffers of eight vertices bound two hemisphere plus two column clips; strict-sign intersections preserve boundary corners once, including exact tangencies.

Packed rows remain three float4 values per sample and area. Sample color.w now stores zero or the one-based area index; parameters[5].w is 8. Existing native upload and fixture writers consume these producer rows unchanged. A new mixed-source test checks indexing/stride. No persisted project schema or Float32 eligibility rules changed.
