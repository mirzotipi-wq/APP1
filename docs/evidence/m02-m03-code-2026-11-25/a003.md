## Implementation and equivalence reasoning

The existing evaluator first checks every active rectangular emitter's finite-face near-field guard. That check is retained in its original position. After identifying an area sample block, the new branch compares the receiver's signed depth against the emitter normal. It skips all 64 interactive or 1,024 reference samples only when the depth is less than `-(0.05 + 1e-9)` meters.

For an ideal planar source, the emitter-normal projection is identical across its face. On this back side, each existing sample's emitted cosine clamps to zero before visibility or material evaluation. Advancing by exactly `grid * grid` therefore preserves all subsequent sample indices and accumulation order.

The cutoff is deliberately more conservative than merely checking a negative depth. Its 5 cm distance keeps the shortcut away from the existing per-sample `r² < 0.0025` unsupported boundary; the additional 1 nm exceeds Double arithmetic/frame rounding at the bounded scene coordinates and emitter dimensions. Consequently, coplanar, almost coplanar and exact 5 cm receivers continue through their original checks. All finite-area near-field errors, including a different near emitter elsewhere in the rig, are checked before any shortcut. Point and directional source blocks retain their original path. Front-facing and grazing receiver sampling, hemisphere clipping, sample ordering, weights, visibility and material evaluation are unchanged.

The evaluator's pixel cancellation sites are unchanged. Eliminating zero-emission work reduces the amount of work between those existing checkpoints; it does not suppress a checkpoint or a final cancellation check.

## Deterministic acceptance

Six new tests cover:

1. Back-facing output across horizontal/vertical/oblique aim, positive/negative roll and large world translations; both full and partial receiver hemispheres, geometric normals and dielectric material retain zero signal and zero visibility calls.
2. Coplanar, small negative/positive plane depths and the exact 5 cm boundary outside the face; an independent unnormalized irradiance formula checks the retained midpoint result.
3. Ordinary front-facing values and a positive 1 mm grazing strip; the latter agrees within 0.2% with the independent diagnostic meter's clipped quadrature.
4. Size-dependent near-field errors behind the emitter and mixed rigs containing a near point or a second near rectangle; none can become a dark valid result.
5. A skipped rectangle inserted before, between and after a visible rectangle/point pair; the complete surface signal remains exactly equal.
6. Back-facing sphere and mannequin renders; cancellation still stops at the third requested callback.

No existing assertion was relaxed or removed.
