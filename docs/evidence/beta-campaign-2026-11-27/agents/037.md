## Findings and evidence

- The filter checks its byte ceiling before copying input, validates the signature, requires the first/only IHDR to have length 13, requires at least one nonempty IDAT, and requires a zero-length terminal IEND with no trailing bytes. Chunk codes must contain ASCII letters; unknown critical chunks fail.
- Length is reconstructed into a 64-bit `Int` on the reviewed host/iOS targets. Before computing the chunk end, the filter compares length with `bytes.count - offset - 12`; this proves the later addition/slice remain within the allocation. Each successful iteration advances at least 12 bytes. Retained output cannot exceed input length. The implementation performs linear filtering and bounded input/output copies; this is source reasoning, not a peak-memory benchmark.
- The actual-source probe deliberately confirmed that invalid CRCs, zero IHDR dimensions/bit depth, invalid zlib payloads, duplicate/misordered PLTE and separated IDAT can survive filtering. These are **parser-only acceptance observations**, not evidence that `ReferencePhoto.sanitize` or ImageIO accepts them. The sole production filter call receives a newly encoded PNG from a new bitmap.
- Import checks the 32 MiB bound before ImageIO, requires one supported still-image source and positive dimensions, and uses `h <= 100_000_000 / w` after bounding positive `w`, avoiding pixel-product overflow. The decoded thumbnail must have positive dimensions and maximum edge 1600 before `width * 4` allocation. Drawing into a new 8-bit sRGB canvas and encoding without source properties separates source metadata from the derivative; the filter additionally discards EXIF/text and all nonallowlisted ancillary chunks. Color-management chunks, including iCCP, are intentionally retained from that fresh encoder output.
- Cancellation checks bracket synchronous decode/draw/filter operations. They prevent publishing cancelled work but do not interrupt an ImageIO call. Decoder metadata expansion, malformed-source CPU/memory behavior and denied/limited photo permissions remain unmeasured platform boundaries.
