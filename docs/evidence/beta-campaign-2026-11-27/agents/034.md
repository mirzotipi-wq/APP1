## Finding and supported domain

**No actionable defect found within the declared scene domain.** Point illuminance applies receiver cosine to candela divided by squared distance; source/receiver coordinates are finite meters within ±100, intensity is 0–100,000 cd, and source distance must be at least 0.05 m. Rectangle intensity denotes total far-field on-axis candela: dividing by face area gives the uniform Lambertian luminance used by the integral. Dimensions are 0.05–4 m, axes are unit/orthogonal, and absolute plane separation must be at least `max(0.05, longestSide / 4)`.

The rectangle implementation preserves these restrictions before its zero-output and subnormal paths. Supported backfaces return zero; unsupported near-plane configurations return nil. Fully positive incidence uses 32×32 midpoint samples; partial incidence uses exact half-plane clipping followed by bounded triangle quadrature. The affine incidence interpolation avoids subtractive loss in narrow contributing regions. These are ideal unoccluded calculations; no equipment, spectral, reflection or physical-calibration conclusion follows.

Target-lux adjustment validates target/source/scene, uses a one-candela forward coefficient, rejects positive targets with unsupported or zero contribution, and checks the actual 100,000-cd forward maximum before inversion. It retains an already exact setting and re-evaluates the returned candidate to a relative `1e-9` gate. Zero skips the photometric sample while retaining source/scene validation. Identity and every field except candela survive adjustment.
